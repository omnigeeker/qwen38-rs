Development Background
Since the Biden administration implemented semiconductor export controls in 2022, NVIDIA has specially designed the H20 chip to comply with export controls to meet the demand in the Chinese market. This is the most powerful among the three GPUs developed by NVIDIA for the Chinese market. Although it is inferior to its flagship AI chips, the H100 and H800, in terms of computing power, interconnect speed, and bandwidth, it adopts the Hopper architecture and still holds significant advantages in the field of inference computing.

Chip Overview
The NVIDIA H20 chip was launched to comply with United States export restrictions and is an AI accelerator specifically designed for the China market. The H20 is based on NVIDIA's Hopper architecture, featuring 96GB of HBM3 memory and providing a memory bandwidth of 4.0 TB/s. In terms of computing power, the H20 delivers an FP8 performance of 296 TFLOPS and an FP16 performance of 148 TFLOPS. Compared to the H100, it has 41% fewer GPU cores and a 28% reduction in performance. The H20 chip has a theoretical maximum cluster size of 50,000 cards, with each card offering 0.148 PetaFLOPS of computing power, resulting in a total of approximately 7400 PetaFLOPS, which is lower than that of the H100/H800 and A100. Consequently, the scale of an H20 cluster falls far short of the theoretical scale achievable with H100 clusters. Based on estimates of the balance between computing power and communication, a reasonable median for overall computing power is around 3000 PetaFLOPS. Significantly more cost and expanded computing power would be required to complete the training of models with hundreds of billions of parameters.

Core Technology
CoWoS Packaging Technology
The H20 utilizes the advanced CoWoS packaging technology. Strictly speaking, CoWoS is a 2.5D advanced packaging technology. It is formed by combining CoW and oS: first, chips are connected to a silicon interposer via the Chip on Wafer (CoW) packaging process, and then the CoW chip is connected to a substrate, integrating them into CoWoS. The core concept involves stacking different chips on the same silicon interposer to achieve multi-chip interconnection. Since its development by TSMC in 2011, CoWoS has undergone five technological iterations. TSMC categorizes CoWoS packaging technology into three types—CoWoS-S, CoWoS-R, and CoWoS-L—each differing in technical characteristics and applications. CoWoS advanced packaging technology is primarily applied in the fields of AI computing chips and HBM. NVIDIA is a major demand driver for CoWoS. Within TSMC's CoWoS production capacity, NVIDIA accounts for over 50% of the total supply.

Hopper Architecture
The Hopper architecture is named after Grace Hopper, known as the "First Lady of Computer Software Engineering." Grace Hopper was one of the pioneers in computer science and invented the world's first compiler—the A-0 System. In March 2022, NVIDIA released its first GPU based on the Hopper architecture—the NVIDIA H100.

Development Timeline
In October 2023, following the U.S. government's ban on the sale of the H800 chip to the China market, NVIDIA launched a "crippled version" of the H20 specifically for the China market, in compliance with the requirements of the U.S. Department of Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security's Export Administration Regulations.
9
In February 2024, NVIDIA began accepting pre-orders from distributors for the new AI chip H20 GPU, which is exclusively supplied to China. Each card was priced between $12,000 and $15,000 (approximately 86,000 to 110,000 yuan), slightly lower than the Ascend chip's price of around 120,000 yuan. The H20 servers provided by distributors were pre-configured with 8 AI chips, with a price of 1.4 million yuan.

Chinese Response
On July 18, 2025, the Ministry of Commerce responded to media inquiries regarding the U.S. approval of sales of NVIDIA 's H20 chips to China. A reporter asked: Recently, relevant U.S. officials stated that the U.S. approval of sales of NVIDIA's H20 chips to China is part of the U.S.-China economic and trade negotiations. Currently, Chinese companies like Huawei have already produced equivalent chips, and the U.S. does not want China to achieve domestic substitution. What is the Ministry of Commerce's comment on this? A spokesperson for the Ministry of Commerce stated that following the U.S.-China London economic and trade talks, the two sides have maintained close communication, confirmed the details of the London framework, and advanced related implementation work. China reviews and approves export applications for controlled items that meet the conditions according to law. In early July, the U.S. side correspondingly lifted the restrictions on China involved in the talks. We have noted that the U.S. side recently proactively indicated it would approve sales of NVIDIA's H20 chips to China. China believes the U.S. side should abandon its zero-sum thinking and continue to remove a series of unreasonable economic and trade restrictions against China. We hope the U.S. side will work with China in the same direction, correct its wrong practices through equal consultations, create a favorable environment for mutually beneficial cooperation between enterprises of the two countries, and jointly maintain the stability of the global semiconductor industry and supply chain.

Chip Vulnerability
In July 2025, NVIDIA 's computing power chips were exposed as having serious security vulnerabilities. Previously, U.S. lawmakers had called for advanced chips exported from the United States to be equipped with "tracking and positioning" capabilities. Experts in the U.S. artificial intelligence field revealed that the "tracking and positioning" and "remote shutdown" technologies for NVIDIA's computing power chips were already mature.
On July 31, 2025, to safeguard the cybersecurity and data security of Chinese users, and in accordance with the relevant provisions of the Cybersecurity Law, the Data Security Law, and the Personal Information Protection Law, the Cyberspace Administration of China summoned NVIDIA for talks. The administration requested NVIDIA to provide explanations and submit relevant supporting materials regarding the security risks and potential backdoors in the H20 computing power chips sold to China.
18
On July 31, 2025, Foreign Ministry Spokesperson Guo Jiakun presided over a regular press conference. During the conference, a foreign media reporter asked: China announced today that it has summoned the U.S. company NVIDIA for talks to discuss serious security issues related to its H20 chips. What is the Foreign Ministry's comment on this? Guo Jiakun responded by stating that for specific questions, please refer to the relevant Chinese competent authorities.

On August 2, 2025, an article pointed out that NVIDIA 's H20 chips were alleged to have potential risks of "tracking and positioning" and "remote shutdown," sparking widespread discussion. Related concerns suggested that such technological vulnerabilities could lead to severe consequences, such as new energy vehicles suddenly losing power while driving at high speed, malfunctions in remote medical equipment, or payment systems failing without cause. In response to the allegations, NVIDIA stated that its products do not contain so-called "backdoors" and cannot be remotely controlled by others. However, this statement has not fully alleviated public concerns.

"Backdoor" Risks
On August 10, 2025, after Tan Zhu investigated the potential backdoors in the H20 chips, NVIDIA responded, stating: "Cybersecurity is of utmost importance to us. NVIDIA's chips do not have 'backdoors' and do not allow anyone to access or control the chips remotely."
Since the Cyberspace Administration of China summoned NVIDIA to express concerns about the security risks and potential backdoors in the H20 computing chip on July 31, 2025, NVIDIA has issued three similar responses, each emphasizing that cybersecurity is of paramount importance to the company and that the chips do not contain "backdoors." Such responses require NVIDIA to substantiate them with further actions. In this regard, the Cyberspace Administration of China also requested NVIDIA to submit relevant supporting documentation during the discussions.
